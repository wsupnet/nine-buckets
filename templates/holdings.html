How cost basis is calculated

Sells are matched against buys oldest-first (FIFO). Fees increase the cost of a buy and reduce the proceeds of a sell. For metals, cost basis is spot + premium, and the premium is tracked separately so you can see what dealer markup has cost you. Weight is normalised to troy ounces, so a 100 g bar and a 1 oz coin sit correctly in the same ledger — and copper, which is quoted per pound upstream, is converted rather than being off by 14.6×. A holding with no lots keeps its manually entered quantity and shows no basis, so you can add ledgers one position at a time.

Moved in / moved out rows come from a holding move on the Assets & Liabilities page and are not trades. The units leave one holding carrying their basis and arrive at the other keeping their original purchase dates, so FIFO still matches the genuinely oldest units first and no gain is realized. If a network fee meant less arrived than was sent, the full basis rides on the smaller quantity, which raises its unit cost — the loss shows up as a lower market value, not as an invented sale.
